Guitar Setup

Setting up your guitar is one of the cheapest and easiest way to improve the sound of your guitar. It also makes your guitar easier to play. Knowing how to setup your own guitar is very important, because not only would you be able to save money, you could also customize it to your own liking. This page is only for electric guitar, because setting up an acoustic guitar is very difficult.

Step 1: Tools Needed

You would need a plier, hex key that is compatible with your truss rod and bridge, capo, new set of guitar strings, guitar tuner. Or you could buy a complete guitar maintenance kit

Step 2: Change Your Strings

Old strings are worn out which would make your setup inaccurate. So changing your string is the first thing you should be doing when setting up your guitar. Cleaning the guitar when the strings are off is recommended because it is more difficult to clean when the strings are on. And before moving on to the next step make sure it is on tune.

Step 3: Adjust Your Truss Rod

This is the hardest step on the whole setup of the guitar, if you’re not comfortable I always recommend on bringing it to your local guitar shop, because breaking the truss rod would mean breaking the guitar.

Doing the tap test is the easiest way to know if you have a back bow or an up bow. To do the tap test you would need a capo on the 1st fret, and you would need to put your finger on the fret where the neck meets the body usually on the 17th or 15th fret. And now you would need to tap the 7th fret while holding the 17th or 15th fret, if there is very little space or no space at all your guitar has a back bow and if there is too much space that when you tap it makes a sound then your guitar has an up bow, but if it has a gap but not too much then your neck relief is perfect and you would not need to change anything.

Make sure to only turn the truss rod in small increment only and always re-tune your guitar when making adjustments.

If your guitar has back bow turn the truss rod with a hex key counter-clockwise and if it has up bow turn it clock-wise.

Step 4: Adjust Your String Height

Setting the string height as low as possible is the goal, while not having any fret buzz. Make sure your guitar is always in tune after adjusting any string height. You would need a hex key that fits your bridge, this is usually included when you buy an electric guitar. First, make the string height as low as possible. Second, make sure that everything is in tune. Third, check every fret of the string that you adjusted, check if there are any fret that sounds incomplete or it has a buzzing sound that is what we call a fret buzz. Fourth, adjust it higher if it has a fret buzz and repeat on every other string.
